What is TQQQ?

TQQQ is ProShares' leveraged ETF that seeks 3x the daily performance of the Nasdaq-100 Index, before fees and expenses. It launched on February 9, 2010 and trades on the Nasdaq.

How does TQQQ work?

TQQQ uses total-return swaps and Nasdaq-100 futures to get about $3 of index exposure for every $1 of fund assets, and resets that leverage every trading day.

Daily reset. The 3x target applies to one day only. At each close the fund rebalances its exposure back to 3x its new net assets, so returns compound daily, and over any period longer than a day they can be higher or lower than 3x the index.

Structure. ProShares' holdings file shows a stock portfolio of Nasdaq-100 members, plus swap contracts with ten bank counterparties (Bank of America, Société Générale, BNP Paribas, Citibank, Barclays, JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, UBS, Nomura, Morgan Stanley), Nasdaq-100 E-mini futures, and Treasury bills and a money-market fund held as collateral. Total swap exposure is roughly 254% of net assets.

Does TQQQ lose value over time?

It can. Because TQQQ resets every day, choppy markets erode its value even when the Nasdaq-100 ends flat. This is called volatility decay, or beta slippage.

Say the index rises 10% and then falls 9.09%: it is back where it started. TQQQ would rise 30% and then fall 27.27%, ending about 5.5% lower. The calculator below shows how much a repeating up-and-down move costs over time.

Can you hold TQQQ long term?

You can, but ProShares says TQQQ is designed for one-day results, and over longer periods returns can differ significantly from 3x. It best fits experienced investors who monitor positions closely.

ProShares notes that smaller index moves and higher volatility tend to make returns worse than 3x, while larger moves and lower volatility tend to make them better. In strong, steady uptrends, long holds have beaten 3x. In volatile or falling markets, losses can be severe and recovery slow.

May suit: active traders, tactical allocators, and investors with high risk tolerance who check positions often. Usually not suited for: retirement core holdings, investors who can't stomach drawdowns of 50% or more, or anyone who doesn't understand daily compounding.

TQQQ FAQ

Is TQQQ a good long-term investment?

TQQQ is designed to deliver 3x the Nasdaq-100's return for a single day. Over longer periods, compounding and volatility can make returns differ significantly from 3x, so it suits active traders who monitor positions rather than buy-and-hold investors.

Can TQQQ go to zero?

A one-day Nasdaq-100 drop of about 33% would theoretically wipe out a 3x daily fund before fees. Such a drop has never happened, but large losses are possible, and the fund can lose most of its value in prolonged declines.

Who issues TQQQ?

TQQQ is issued by ProShares and launched on February 9, 2010. It trades on the Nasdaq.

What is the difference between TQQQ and SQQQ?

TQQQ targets +3x the Nasdaq-100's daily return; SQQQ targets −3x. TQQQ rises when the index rises; SQQQ rises when it falls.

Does TQQQ pay dividends?

Yes, TQQQ pays small quarterly distributions. Trailing-12-month dividends were about $0.48 per share (about 0.64% yield) as of October 14, 2026, per StockAnalysis.

What is TQQQ's expense ratio?

TQQQ's net expense ratio is 0.82% and its gross expense ratio is 0.97%, per ProShares.
